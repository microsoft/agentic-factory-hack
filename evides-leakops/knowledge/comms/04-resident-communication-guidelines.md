# COMM-STYLE-01: Writing public notices for drinking-water disruptions
Versie 2.1 (gesimuleerd) | Owner: Communication (simulated)

## Tone and language
- **Dutch first, then English.** Dutch at B1 level, short sentences, address people with **"u"**. English is a natural translation, not a copy.
- Say what residents notice (no or little water), where (place and streets) and, when it helps, roughly how many connections.
- Be honest about what is known: "Er is een storing in de waterleiding. Vermoedelijk is er een leidingbreuk." Use "vermoedelijk" / "suspected" for the cause.
- Always say that Evides is working on it and that the page is kept up to date.

## Never write
- Internal ids or codes, pipe material, diameter, age, valves, technician names, costs, severity codes.
- A repair time, duration or arrival before the schedule exists. The system adds the time later in a fixed template.
- Blame, speculation about the cause, or the name of a customer. Vulnerable customers are taken into account, but are never named.
- Anything about whether the water is safe, drinkable, clean or contaminated, or what to do with the water. That is health advice and is added by the system or given by the help line.
- Promises. Do not write "technicians are on their way" until the schedule says so.

## Length
Title at most 90 characters. Message at most 500 characters. The advice field is left empty: the system adds the standard advice and the help line (0900 0787).

## Examples of good sentences
- NL: "In Spijkenisse hebben bewoners aan de Dorpsweg mogelijk geen of weinig water. Evides werkt aan een oplossing. Deze pagina wordt bijgehouden met nieuwe informatie."
- EN: "In Spijkenisse, residents on Dorpsweg may have no water or low water pressure. Evides is working on a solution. This page will be updated with new information."

## Status words used on the page
reported (Melding ontvangen), repair planned (Reparatie gepland), repair in progress (Reparatie bezig), resolved (Opgelost).
